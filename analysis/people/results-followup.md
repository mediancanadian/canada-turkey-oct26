# Canada and Türkiye: follow-up on flights, the air agreement and work permits

Prepared 2026-10-23. This adds to results.md in the same folder. Raw files are in raw/followup/, scripts in scripts/followup/, and the tidy output is outputs/work_permit_split_turkey.json. SHA-256 hashes are in manifest-followup.json. All sources were accessed 2026-10-23.

## 1. CTA rulings on Turkish Airlines capacity

I searched the CTA decisions database (otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/decisions-determinations) by title for "Turkish Airlines" and "Türk Hava", and by content for "Turkish Airlines", "Türkiye" and "Istanbul" from 2025-06-01 to 2026-10-23. It lists four extra-bilateral rulings for Turkish Airlines passenger flights:

| Ruling | Date | Request | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| [A-2024-45](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/ruling/a-2024-45) (case 24-14877) | 2024-04-08 | +1 weekly Istanbul to Toronto, May 1 to Sep 30, 2024 (13 total) | Granted |
| [A-2024-140](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/ruling/a-2024-140) (case 24-46454) | 2024-09-17 | Extension of the same flight to Oct 31, 2024 | Granted |
| [A-2025-25](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/ruling/a-2025-25) (case 24-66305) | 2025-02-14 | +5 weekly (1 Toronto, 2 Montréal, 2 Vancouver), Apr 1 to Oct 31, 2025 (17 total). Filed 2024-12-12 | Denied |
| [A-2025-77](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/ruling/a-2025-77) (case 25-13225) | 2025-04-29 | +1 weekly Istanbul to Toronto, Jun 1 to Oct 31, 2025 | Denied |

What the rulings say:
- The 2009 agreement is "initialled ad referendum on March 13, 2009, as amended". It gives each country 12 weekly flights in each direction on its own aircraft. Turkish carriers may fly 9 of these east of the Ontario-Manitoba border (Toronto and Montréal) and 3 west of it (Vancouver), per A-2024-45.
- Turkish Airlines was flying all 12: six to Toronto, three to Montréal and three to Vancouver (A-2024-45 and A-2025-77). A-2025-77 also says: "No Canadian air carriers are currently operating any of the twelve weekly flights that they are allocated pursuant to the Agreement."
- Reason for the A-2025-25 denial: "granting a further extension of extra-bilateral authority to Turkish Airlines would substantially change the negotiated regime of the Agreement outside the ongoing negotiation process" and "it would be inappropriate to go beyond what was negotiated by the two countries".
- Reason for the A-2025-77 denial: the December 2023 talks added cargo flights but no passenger flights, and Turkish Airlines had "not filed any compelling new evidence or arguments that would justify granting the exceptional authority ... for a second consecutive year".

Correction to results.md (and to the ch-aviation, Travel Extra and aviation.direct leads): Canadian airports did not oppose the request. The official A-2025-25 text says:

| Party | A-2025-25 (+5 weekly) | A-2025-77 (+1 weekly) |
|---|---|---|
| Air Canada | Opposed. Called the 42% increase "unreasonably excessive" | Opposed (res judicata) |
| Air Transat | Opposed | No intervention recorded |
| Aéroports de Montréal | Supported | Not notified |
| GTAA (Toronto Pearson) | Supported. Cited demand up 49% in 2023 vs 2019 | Notified, no intervention recorded |
| Vancouver Airport Authority | Notified, did not intervene | Not notified |
| WestJet | Not notified | Supported |

The opposition came from the two Canadian airlines designated on the route. The two biggest airports supported the request.

Authority for 2026: the database has no 2026 ruling that lets Turkish Airlines fly more than 12 passenger flights a week. Its only 2026 Turkish Airlines ruling is [A-2026-59](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/ruling/a-2026-59) (2026-04-10, case 26-23513). That ruling approves an all-cargo wet lease with Atlas Air from May 7, 2026 to May 7, 2027. It cites a cargo "Arrangement ... set out in an Agreed Minute signed on February 7, 2020", and its predecessor is [A-2025-81](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/ruling/a-2025-81) (2025-05-06). The trade press reports more than 12 Turkish Airlines flights in summer 2026. If so, that capacity most likely rests on the revised arrangement reached in May 2026 being applied administratively, which needs no CTA ruling. No official page states this, so it is an inference. Transport Canada "ad hoc" permits do not appear in the CTA database, and I found none published.

2026 Turkish Airlines frequencies: I found no official source. turkishairlines.com timed out on every try, and the CTA wet-lease portal blocks automated access. These figures are trade press only (leads):
- Travelweek (2026-06-02) and AeroRoutes (2026-07-01): summer 2026 is Toronto 7, Montréal 4 and Vancouver 4 weekly. For winter 2026/27, Montréal and Vancouver go to 5 weekly from Dec 7 and Toronto is 7 weekly from Dec 12.
- Aviation A2Z (2026-05-16): Montréal goes from 3 to 4 weekly from July 7, 2026, and Vancouver gets four extra round trips in June 2026.

## 2. The new Canada-Türkiye Air Transport Agreement

Official Canadian statements:
- PMO news release, 2026-10-23, [Prime Minister Carney's historic visit to Türkiye launches new Strategic Partnership](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/10/23/prime-minister-carneys-historic-visit-turkiye-launches-new-strategic). It says "the two countries signed the recently expanded Air Transport Agreement, which increases weekly passenger flights from 12 to up to 21 and allows unlimited cargo flights". Its backgrounder adds: "allows unlimited access to all points in each country, up to 21 weekly passenger-combination flights per country, and unlimited weekly all-cargo flights."
- PM remarks, 2026-10-22, [Carney alongside Erdoğan](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/speeches/2026/10/22/prime-minister-carney-delivers-remarks-alongside-president-turkiye-recep): "we are formalising an air transport agreement to increase passenger flights from 12 a week to 21, with no limit on cargo flights."
- Global Affairs media advisory, issued 2026-08-14, [Minister Sidhu to make announcement related to Canada-Türkiye Air Transport Agreement](https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2026/08/minister-sidhu-to-make-announcement-related-to-canadaturkiye-air-transport-agreement.html). The event was set for Aug 16, 2026, apparently at Toronto Pearson. I found no follow-up canada.ca release. The @CanadaTrade post on X (official account, but social media) says passenger flights go "from 12 to 21" with "unlimited all-cargo flights".
- Order in Council 2026-0976 (2026-10-09) authorizes signing an air transport agreement with Türkiye. This comes from a Bloomberg Tax lead. The OIC database search returned no rows, so I could not view the order myself.
- The CTA agreement page still shows the 2009 agreement as "Applied administratively" (date modified 2009-07-31). No treaty text was found on Canada Treaty Information.

Türkiye side: I could not reach SHGM (web.shgm.gov.tr timed out). These are leads only:
- Haber.aero and Airlinehaber (2026-05-12) report a memorandum of understanding between SHGM and Canadian authorities, with 12 rising to 21 weekly. Turkville (2026-05-10) reports unlimited cargo with fifth-freedom rights.
- I found no statement from the Ministry of Transport (UAB).

Conclusion: the 21-a-week figure is now confirmed by two official Canadian PMO sources. Note that Canada's wording is "up to 21 ... per country".

## 3. Work permits held by citizens of Türkiye, by purpose

Sources (IRCC open data, catalogue [360024f2](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/360024f2-17e9-4558-bfc1-3616485d65b9)):
- [IMP holders on Dec 31, all purposes](https://www.ircc.canada.ca/opendata-donneesouvertes/data/en_odp_annual-tr-work-imp_citz_year_end_all_purpose.xlsx)
- [IMP holders on Dec 31, for work purposes only](https://www.ircc.canada.ca/opendata-donneesouvertes/data/en_odp_annual-tr-work-imp_citz_year_end.xlsx). Per IRCC's note, this file excludes "IMP work permit holders who may be in Canada for reasons other than primarily working (e.g. for asylum claimants or Humanitarian and Compassionate reasons)".
- [Work permits for Humanitarian & Compassionate purposes by citizenship](https://www.ircc.canada.ca/opendata-donneesouvertes/data/EN_ODP-TR-Work-HC_citizenship_sign.xlsx)

Values are rounded to 5. Year-end files run only to 2024.

Holders on December 31:

| Year | IMP, all purposes | IMP, work only | Difference (asylum, H&C and similar) | TFWP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 4,995 | 1,660 | 3,335 | 145 |
| 2022 | 9,185 | 2,315 | 6,870 | 230 |
| 2023 | 24,740 | 11,250 | 13,490 | 180 |
| 2024 | 27,500 | 13,640 | 13,860 | 175 |
| Change, 2022 to 2024 | +18,315 | +11,325 (62%) | +6,990 (38%) | -55 |

Permits that became effective in each year:

| Year | H&C-purpose work permits | IMP, work only | TFWP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 4,035 | 1,615 | 165 |
| 2023 | 8,235 | 10,640 | 270 |
| 2024 | 3,100 | 4,620 | 85 |
| 2025 | 2,155 | 1,425 | 100 |
| 2026 Jan to Aug | 1,230 | 1,795 | 70 |

(The IMP "effective" file used in results.md was already the work-only version, according to its own notes.)

How much of the jump is linked to asylum claims or the earthquake measures:
- About 38% of the 2022 to 2024 rise in IMP holders is permits held for non-work purposes (asylum claimants, H&C). This share follows the asylum surge: 9,465 claimants in 2022 and 6,365 in 2023.
- The other 62% (the work-only rise) is not a rise in job-offer hiring. Employer-specific LMIA permits (TFWP) stayed flat at 165 to 270 a year.
- Work-only IMP permits for Türkiye citizens were concentrated in a few months: Aug 2023 740, Sep 4,075, Oct 2,375, Nov 925, Dec 1,700, and Jan 2024 2,695. That is about 12,510 in six months, compared with 190 to 320 a month earlier in 2023. They then fell to 100 to 460 a month from Feb 2024.
- This window matches IRCC's earthquake public policies under IRPA s. 25.2. These covered fee-exempt open work permits for Türkiye and Syria nationals already in Canada with valid status. The first policy was signed 2023-03-15 and took effect 2023-03-29 ([policy](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/mandate/policies-operational-instructions-agreements/public-policies/turkiye-syria-earthquake-2023.html)). The extension was signed 2023-09-28 and expired 2024-01-03 ([extension](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/mandate/policies-operational-instructions-agreements/public-policies/turkiye-syria-earthquake-2023-extension.html); [instructions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/publications-manuals/operational-bulletins-manuals/service-delivery/earthquake-turkiye-syria.html), coded R01 "A25.2 Public Policy").
- IRCC publishes no count of permits issued under the earthquake policy, and no split by citizenship of open versus employer-specific IMP permits. The earthquake link is therefore an inference from timing, not a published figure.

Safe wording: "Most of the 2023 to 2024 jump in work permits held by Turkish citizens came from permits held while an asylum claim was pending, and from open permits issued in late 2023 under the earthquake relief policy. Permits tied to a job offer barely moved."
