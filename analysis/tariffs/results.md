### 1d. Exactly what applies to Turkish steel in 2026

1. MFN tariff: Free on all 2025 iron and steel imports from Türkiye in Chapter 72, and on 95% of Chapter 73 value [S1][S3].
2. Steel TRQ surtax (Order Imposing a Surtax on the Importation of Certain Steel Goods, SOR/2025-148 as amended by SOR/2025-155, SOR/2025-266 and SOR/2026-119) [S11][S12]:
   - Türkiye is not in Schedule 3 ("Countries with Trade Agreement"), so it is a non-FTA country under Schedule 1 [S11].
   - 23 product classes. Imports above the quarterly quota, or above the single-country share of it, pay a 50% surtax on value for duty, on top of MFN duty and any SIMA duties [S12].
   - From 2025-12-26, non-FTA tariff-free quota volumes were cut to 20% of 2024 levels (non-CUSMA FTA partners: 75%) [S12].
   - SOR/2026-119 extended the regime to 2027-06-27 with new volumes from 2026-06-28 [S12]. Year 2 quarters: 2026-06-28 to 2026-09-29, 2026-09-30 to 2026-12-29, 2026-12-30 to 2027-03-29, 2027-03-30 to 2027-06-27 [S13].
   - Examples of non-FTA quarterly quota from 2026-06-28 (all non-FTA countries combined): rebar 13,445.1 t (single-country cap 29%); standard pipe 10,369.2 t (21%); coated sheet 18,732.8 t (37%); steel plate 5,201.2 t (36%) [S13].
   - Tariff-free entry needs a GAC shipment-specific import permit; without one the surtax applies automatically [S12].
   - 2025 imports from Türkiye in tariff lines now in the TRQ classes: CA$220.1M, led by standard pipe CA$52.4M, steel plate CA$50.0M, structural steel CA$27.6M, OCTG CA$22.9M [S1][S11] (calc).
3. Steel derivative goods surtax (SOR/2025-267, from 2025-12-26): 25% on listed derivative items (for example 7308 structures, 7312 to 7318 wire products and fasteners, some furniture) from all countries [S14][S15]. Non-stackable: it does not apply if the TRQ surtax applies [S14][S15]. Exemptions include goods for motor vehicle or aircraft manufacture imported before 2027-07-01, and wind towers for projects west of the Ontario-Manitoba border [S15][S19]. 2025 imports from Türkiye in these items: CA$77.8M, of which towers and lattice masts (7308.20) CA$38.1M [S1][S15] (calc).
4. China melt-and-pour surtax (Steel Goods and Aluminum Goods Surtax Order, from 2025-07-31): 25% on listed goods containing steel melted and poured in China, whatever the exporting country, so it can reach Turkish goods made from Chinese-melted steel; goods are deemed to contain it if the importer cannot prove otherwise [S22].
5. Anti-dumping and countervailing duties: the 8 steel measures in table 1c [S7].
6. Only one of the surtaxes applies to a good (order of precedence: TRQ surtax, then US/China/melt-and-pour surtaxes, then derivative surtax) [S14].

UNCONFIRMED: how much TRQ surtax and derivative surtax was actually paid on Turkish steel in 2025 to 2026. I found no CBSA or GAC table of surtax by country.

## 3. The EU customs union gap

- Scope: the EU-Türkiye Customs Union (Decision 1/95, final phase from 1996-01-01) covers industrial goods and processed agricultural products. Türkiye applies the EU Common Customs Tariff to third countries. Agriculture, coal and steel are covered by separate preferential deals, not the customs union [S28][S29][S27].
- Free circulation: third-country goods that have cleared customs in the EU are "in free circulation" and can move to Türkiye with an A.TR movement certificate. A.TR proves free circulation, not origin. Certain agricultural products qualify only if they originate in the EU or Türkiye [S29][S30].
- Türkiye is not party to CETA. EU FTAs bind only the EU. Under Article 16 of Decision 1/95, Türkiye negotiates its own FTAs with EU FTA partners [S29]. Türkiye has 22 FTAs in force, including EFTA, the UK, South Korea, Singapore, Malaysia and Chile, and none with Canada [S28].
- Effect for Canada: CETA removed EU duties on 99% of tariff lines [S31]. A Canadian industrial good can enter the EU duty-free under CETA, be released into free circulation and move to Türkiye on an A.TR without Turkish customs duty [S29][S30]. Shipped directly, the same good pays Türkiye's MFN rate, which equals the EU external tariff.
- Türkiye's partial counter-measure: Article 2(2) of the IGV decision (Karar 3351) applies the Other Countries IGV rate to goods that come in on an A.TR but are not of EU or Turkish origin, unless preferential origin within a cumulation system is proven [S21]. The same rule applies to the extra financial charge in List II footnotes [S20]. So the EU route avoids the customs duty but not the IGV on IGV-listed lines.
- Türkiye's official position: in the 2023 WTO Trade Policy Review, "the Turkish authorities indicated that ... some of the European Union's RTA partners have been reluctant to negotiate RTAs with Türkiye, as once their industrial products have entered the EU market, they can then enter the Turkish market free of customs duties. They emphasized the need to address this asymmetric structure of the Customs Union." [S27]. Türkiye's Directorate for EU Affairs lists "risks of trade diversion and unfair competition due to the reluctance of some of EU's free trade agreement (FTA) partner countries to conclude similar agreements with Türkiye" [S29].
- Agriculture: pulses and wheat fall outside the customs union, so the EU route gives no advantage. The EU itself pays the same 19.3% on lentils and 130% on durum wheat as Canada [S20]. Pulses are covered only by Türkiye's own tariff.
- Canada's side (see 1b): on almost all tariff items where Turkish goods paid duty in 2025, EU and UK goods enter Canada free under CETA and CUKTCA [S3] (calc).
- Modernisation: the Commission proposed modernising the Customs Union in December 2016; the Council has not adopted negotiating directives [S28].
- UNCONFIRMED: the volume of Canadian goods actually reaching Türkiye through the EU. No official source quantifies it.

## 4. Estimated duty paid (estimates)

Method: 2025 trade values times 2026 applied rates. Scripts: `scripts/05_ca_duty_estimate.py`, `scripts/06_tr_duty_estimate.py`. Outputs in `outputs/`.

### Canada on imports from Türkiye

- Estimated MFN customs duty: about CA$105.3M on CA$3,197.3M of imports, 3.3% of value (estimate) [S1][S3]. On dutiable goods alone, about 10.0%.
- Duty-free share: 67.1% of 2025 import value had a Free MFN rate [S1][S3] (calc).
- Concentration: clothing (Chapters 61 and 62) about CA$38.3M and carpets about CA$14.1M account for about half of the estimated duty [S1][S3] (calc).
- Not included: SIMA duties, steel surtaxes, duty relief and drawback, Chapter 98 and 99 concessions, remission orders. Specific rates were applied only where StatCan quantity is in kg. CA$1.5M of value had no parsable rate.
- Published figure check: I found no CBSA or StatCan table of customs duties collected by country of origin. UNCONFIRMED that none exists.

### Türkiye on Canada's exports

- Estimated customs duty plus IGV: about CA$101.9M on CA$1,029.4M of domestic exports, 9.9% of value (estimate) [S2][S20][S21].
- Durum wheat at 130% gives about CA$37.2M of the total. Excluding durum: about CA$64.4M, 6.4% (estimate).
- Pulses: lentils about CA$30.9M and chickpeas about CA$3.5M (estimate). Using the 10% green-lentil rate in force to 2026-04-30 would lower the green-lentil figure from about CA$8.0M to about CA$4.1M [S20].
- Duty-free share: 45.3% of 2025 export value faces 0% (customs duty plus IGV) [S2][S20][S21] (calc). This assumes the civil-aircraft 0% end-use relief for aircraft and aircraft engines; without it, aircraft lines would pay 2.7%.
- Caveats: (a) imports under Türkiye's inward processing regime enter without customs duty if used in exports [S27]. Durum wheat for pasta exports may use this, so the wheat figure is likely an overstatement (UNCONFIRMED share). (b) Turkish duty is charged on CIF value, while StatCan export values are Canadian export valuations, which understates duty. (c) 19.4% of value was matched as an average of several 12-digit Turkish lines. (d) Specific per-tonne charges are excluded. (e) Turkish pulse and grain duties change often by decree; the rates here are those in force at the file dates.

## 5. Official consultations and stated goals

- GAC consultation: ran 2026-07-31 to 2026-09-14 [S32]. Canada Gazette Part I notice of 2026-08-01, "Consulting Canadians on future trade negotiations with Türkiye" [S34].
- The notice gives 2025 two-way merchandise trade as $4.3B, Canadian exports $1.1B and imports from Türkiye $3.2B. It lists top exports (lentils, aircraft, alloy steel waste and scrap, optical instruments) and top imports (pharmaceutical products, fishing vessels, rugs and textiles, turbine engine components) [S34]. Its topics include tariffs "and any import sensitivities", rules of origin, non-tariff barriers, sanitary and phytosanitary measures, and "Preferred approach to trade remedies" [S34].
- Supply management: neither the consultation page nor the Gazette notice mentions dairy, poultry or eggs [S32][S34] (text search). Since 2025-06-26, the Department of Foreign Affairs, Trade and Development Act s. 10(2.1) says the Minister "must not make any commitment ... that would have the effect of (a) increasing the tariff rate quota ... applicable to dairy products, poultry or eggs; or (b) reducing the tariff applicable to those goods when they are imported in excess of the applicable tariff rate quota" [S33]. 2025 imports of dairy products from Türkiye (HS 0404, 0406) were CA$1.9M, mostly cheese within access commitment [S1].
- Timeline: exploratory talks announced 2026-06-08; FTA negotiations launched 2026-07-07 in Ankara [S34][S35]. On 2026-10-22, the leaders launched the Türkiye-Canada Strategic Partnership and a three-year Action Plan, agreeing "to work toward successful conclusion of a Türkiye - Canada Free Trade Agreement" with priority sectors "energy supply, defence production, aerospace, and AI" [S36].
- Türkiye's stated goal: President Erdoğan said trade "currently stands at some $3 billion" and they hope to raise it "to $5 billion at the first stage, and then to $10 billion" [S37]. The currency is presumably US$; Canada's CA$4.3B figure [S34] uses a different currency and measure. UNCONFIRMED: any Turkish official document setting tariff-specific FTA goals for Canada. I found none.
- A summary of consultation submissions has not yet been published ("A report summarizing the feedback ... will be published") [S32].

## 6. Non-tariff issues

Türkiye, on Canadian goods:
- Pulses: dried pulses (07.13) are on Ek-7 (agricultural quarantine control) of the Ministry of Trade communiqué on imports under Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry control (Ürün Güvenliği ve Denetimi 2026/5). Plant food and feed products in Chapters 07, 10 and 12 are on Ek-2. Imports need a conformity letter (Uygunluk Yazısı) from the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry. 07.13, 10.01 and 12.01 are not marked as needing a pest risk analysis [S38].
- Lentils and chickpeas are on Türkiye's export registration list, and Türkiye has used temporary export bans on lentils (from 2022-02-26) [S27]. This affects Turkish re-exports, not Canadian imports.
- GMOs (soybeans, canola): Law No. 5977 on Biosafety. Since the Biosafety Board was abolished in 2018, approvals are made by the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry and are valid for 10 years. As of end-October 2022 there were 45 approvals for maize, soybeans and GM micro-organisms [S27]. The FAO GM platform lists 15 GM soybean and 21 maize approvals, all for feed import, no applications for food use, and no canola event [S39]. UNCONFIRMED: whether any GM canola event is pending. The official list (tbbdm.gov.tr) was not reachable from this environment.
- Product safety: TAREKS risk-based import checks. Canada, Mexico and the United States raised specific trade concerns at the WTO on Türkiye's toy inspection requirements [S27].

Canada, on Turkish goods (officially documented):
- SIMA measures (1c) and steel surtaxes (1d), including the TRQ import permit requirement and country of melt and pour reporting [S7][S12][S22].
- UNCONFIRMED and not researched: CFIA or Health Canada measures specific to Turkish food products. I found no official Canada-specific SPS restriction on Turkish dried fruit, nuts or pasta in this pass.

## Flags and limits

- Turkish rates are from Ministry of Trade consolidated Excel files, which are working consolidations. The legal texts are the Resmî Gazete decisions (Karar 10790, 10791 and later amendments such as Karar 11506 to 11508 of 2026-07-11) [S23][S24][S40]. I spot-checked the lentil sublines against the scanned 2026 tariff schedule (Karar 10781) [S26].
- CITT continuation of COR2: date taken from a customs-broker republication of the CITT notice (lead source); the case number RR-2025-004 is from that page and was not checked on citt-tcce.gc.ca [S9].
- All duty figures apply 2026 rates to 2025 trade. They are estimates, not collections.
- Trade values: StatCan CIMT bulk files, customs basis, released 2026-07 [S1][S2]. A UN Comtrade check gave Canada's 2025 imports from Türkiye as US$2.29B and exports US$0.81B (cross-check only, not used).
