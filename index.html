Talking trade with Türkiye

A Canadian prime minister's first bilateral visit brought agreements on flights, energy and defence. Here is what they mean for Canadians.

The visit, October 22 and 23, 2026

What came out of the visit

The first bilateral visit to Türkiye by a Canadian prime minister.2,5 Tap an item for what it does.

  • A three-year action plan in four areas: defence, energy, the economy, and social and diplomatic ties. The foreign ministers oversee it, with a strategic dialogue every year.1

  • Signed in Ankara. Each country's airlines may run up to 21 passenger flights a week, up from 12, to any city in the other country. Cargo flights have no limit.2,3

  • The two energy ministers signed a joint statement on liquefied natural gas (LNG), nuclear power, renewables, clean technology and critical minerals. It has no commitments to buy or sell.2,4

  • A memorandum that connects Canadian and Turkish defence companies. It builds on a statement of intent on defence cooperation signed in July 2026.1,2

  • Export Development Canada and Türk Eximbank signed a memorandum to help Canadian and Turkish companies team up on projects in other countries.2,3

  • The first round runs from November 30 to December 4, 2026, in Ankara.2

  • The leaders called for starting a dialogue on mutual legal assistance in criminal cases and extradition.1

  • A Canadian trade mission to Türkiye in 2027, led by the trade minister, and an invitation for President Erdoğan to visit Canada.1,2

1The opportunity

Türkiye is a big market where Canada sells little

Türkiye imported US$365 billion of goods in 2025, and 0.3% came from Canada.6

Canada sold 72% of its goods to the United States in 2025, its smallest share since at least 1999. Canada's government wants to double sales to other countries within a decade.7,8

2Trade deficit

Canada buys more from Türkiye than it sells

The trade deficit was $2.1 billion in 2025. It shrank by almost a quarter from January to August 2026.11

Canada sold Türkiye $1.14 billion of goods in 2025 and bought $3.20 billion. The last year Canada sold more than it bought was 2011. From January to August 2026, Canada sold 8% more to Türkiye and bought 14% less than a year earlier.11

3Duties

A trade deal could help farmers and shoppers

Türkiye charges 19.3% on Canadian lentils, and Canada about 17% on Turkish clothing.12,13

On the Canadian-made goods sold to Türkiye in 2025, durum wheat aside, duties come to an estimated $64 million, 6.4% on average. On Turkish goods sold to Canada, they come to about $105 million, 3.3%. Lower duties could also bring Canadian clothing makers more competition.12,13,14,15

Estimated duty by product, 2025 trade at 2026 rates

Each product line's 2025 value times its 2026 duty rate. Canadian goods leave out durum wheat, which can enter Türkiye duty-free when used to make exports.16 Türkiye's 10% rate on green lentils ended on April 30, 2026; since May 1, green lentils pay 19.3%, like red lentils.12 Canada's figure uses its regular rates; anti-dumping duties and surtaxes on some steel come on top.13

4Who sells

Three provinces have the most at stake

Quebec, Ontario and Saskatchewan make three-quarters of Canada's sales to Türkiye.11

Saskatchewan's lentil sales to Türkiye fell by almost two-thirds from 2022 to 2025.11

5Travel

Canadians get more choice of flights

Istanbul is a global hub: Turkish Airlines flew to 120 countries in 2024, more than any other airline.18

The new agreement allows up to 21 flights a week for each country, up from 12, to any city. Istanbul Airport handled 84.4 million passengers in 2025, eighth in the world.2,19

6Energy

Türkiye's nuclear plans open a door for Canada

Türkiye aims for over 20 gigawatts of nuclear power by 2050 and is studying Canada's CANDU reactors.28,29

Türkiye's first nuclear plant, Akkuyu (4.8 gigawatts), is being built by Russia's Rosatom. Türkiye also plans 120 gigawatts of wind and solar power by 2035 and net zero emissions by 2053. In 2024, Canada produced about a quarter of the world's uranium.30,31,32,33

7Defence

Military sales to Türkiye are back, with conditions

Canada's government says linking the two defence industries will create jobs and exports.2

Türkiye has NATO's second-largest military. Canada's military exports to Türkiye are mostly imaging and targeting equipment.2,38,39

    How this was done

    In plain words

    Every number on this page comes from an official or primary source. The numbers built from data tables are computed by the scripts in the GitHub repository, which also holds the full method (METHODOLOGY.md) with a table of the numbers in the slides and the chapter text. News stories were used only to find official documents.

    • Trade: Statistics Canada's customs records by country, province and product, and Türkiye's own import records from UN Comtrade.
    • Duties: the 2026 Canadian Customs Tariff and Türkiye's 2026 import regime, applied line by line to 2025 trade.
    • Travel and people: Türkiye's border statistics, Canadian Transportation Agency rulings, open data from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) and the 2021 Census.
    • Energy: Türkiye's energy ministry, EPİAŞ (Türkiye's energy exchange), the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), Türkiye's energy regulator, the Canada Energy Regulator and Natural Resources Canada.
    • Defence: Global Affairs Canada's Reports on Exports of Military Goods for 2010 to 2025 and its notices to exporters.

    Caveats

    • Two sets of trade records. Türkiye records what it imports from Canada in US dollars when goods arrive; Canada records what it exports in Canadian dollars when goods leave. The totals differ for timing, valuation and currency.
    • Duties are estimates. Türkiye charges duty on the value including freight and insurance, which Canadian export values leave out, so the Canadian-goods figure is a low estimate. Duties charged per tonne are left out.
    • Military exports. The reports count permits used, and leave out most exports to the United States.
    • 2026 is preliminary. Monthly trade for 2026 can still be revised.
